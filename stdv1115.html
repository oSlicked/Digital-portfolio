Reflection

Me as a digital learner.

When I first got into computers I had no idea what I was doing. I bought a PC from some guy on Kijiji, went home and plugged it in. It worked fine until I discovered what overclocking was. Let’s just say it resulted in me needing to take the computer parts out and replace the motherboard. But in doing so, it taught me how to disassemble a computer and reassemble it all on my own with a screwdriver and a YouTube video.

It reminds me of when I first started working on cars. Trial and error, haha. Sitting on the garage floor with a flashlight in one hand and my phone in the other, watching a ChrisFix video on how to fix what I had just messed up.

At the end of the day, you gotta mess around a little bit to see what works and what doesn’t. If you don’t, you will never truly figure out what it means to be a learner.

Course work

What I worked on this term.

My STDV 1115 research and reflections this term, plus the one line from each that I would keep.

Digital privacy

Protecting the Footprint

How digital privacy shows up in my everyday life. I use Apple’s Hide My Email, and I caught a fake eTransfer phishing link while selling headphones on Kijiji. It also covers how companies like Amazon use our data to change what we pay. My mind map splits it into four parts: scammer manipulation, influencer impersonation, marketplace phishing and price experiments.

“Digital privacy is not simply a personal choice, but something essential.”

Digital learning

Users vs Learners

I compared two articles on screen time and ran both through the CRAAP test. Passive scrolling and active screen time are not the same thing, and it made me rethink how I use my devices.

“Every digital learner is a user, but not every user is a learner.”

Media literacy

Guarding Democracy

I researched foreign interference in Canada using two CBC articles, checked them with the CRAAP test and broke down a political cartoon. Disinformation is built to manipulate you, so media has to be decoded, not just consumed.

“Applying these critical thinking skills ensures citizens are not just tools of the mass media.”

AI and integrity

The Digital Currency of Grades

Simon Lewsen says grades work like currency. As an online student with AI one click away, I argued that schools should teach students to use AI the right way instead of only trying to catch cheaters.

“Taking the easy way out doesn’t build any practical or transferable real world skills.”

Digital wellbeing

Ctrl, Alt, Disconnect

Notes on talks by Naomi Shimada and Peachy Liv about self worth, online toxicity and burnout. I wrote about going semi analog with an A5 notebook and a rOtring 600, and heading to the mountains with only a film camera.

“The best way to find digital harmony is knowing exactly when to leave it behind.”

Reflection

Looking back on the term.

At the start of the term, I gave any website my information without a second thought. After reading how companies track our data and sell it to other companies, I think twice now. These days I sign up with Apple’s Hide My Email, so I still get into the site but my real email stays protected behind a private address.

The assignment that changed my mind the most was the one on AI. Tools like Claude, Gemini and ChatGPT make research a lot faster. Instead of spending two hours going through articles, I send an AI agent out to find sources and report back. I still review what it finds and change the search from there, because the judgment has to stay mine.

One of my week 2 readings treated gaming as wasted screen time, and I went along with it at the time. Looking back, I don’t fully agree. Without gaming I wouldn’t be half the computer person I am today. It taught me almost everything I know about computers, from troubleshooting to writing programs.

Building this site changed my view the most. Animations, clean code and quick fixes take developers years of trial and error to get right. With the help of a Claude agent, the code came together in about three days. I brought the ideas, the photos and the direction, and Claude wrote the code to match.

Looking back, there isn’t a single thing I would change about the site. The journey was the fun part. The final product represents everything that had gone into building this site: all of the ideas, all of the late nights, all of the binging YouTube videos for ideas to make it better.

You can’t force perfection.

How I treat people through a screen, whether it is a stranger on Marketplace or a parent at a tournament.

  

    
Think before you send
From Peachy Liv’s talk, I use one question before I reply online: how would I feel if I were the one getting this comment? On Reddit or Facebook Marketplace, I draft my reply, read it over once or twice, and only then send it.
Framework
Empathy first, and personal accountability for the culture I help create online.
